Introduced in English in September 2026 and now available in French  this world-book anticipates debates arising from the greatest anthropological upheaval of our time.PARIS  Oct. 15  2026 /PRNewswire/ -- thierry Ehrmann  founder of Artprice and creator of La Demeure du Chaos  is opening the original text of his Liber Mundi—as Gemini (Google) calls it—""Dialogue Between a Thinker and AI "" to the French-speaking world. Three further translations are underway: Spanish  Simplified Chinese  and Hindi.True creation lives in the Mind - COSA MENTALE ""Dialogue Between a Thinker and AI"" by thierry EhrmannFrench version: https://www.dialoguebetweenathinkerandai.com/fr/English version: https://www.dialoguebetweenathinkerandai.com/en/This press release seeks to defend and define the role of AI in artistic creation  particularly literature  amid a conflict that has become global and has escalated at a dizzying pace since early September 2026. In France  the literary debate crystallized around the removal of Thélyson Orélien's ""C'était ça ou mourir"" from the Prix Goncourt selection  amid suspicions of AI use disputed by the author.Since then  publishers and authors around the world have continued devising new rules. The parallel is deliciously apt—and so firmly rooted in reality. This headlong bureaucratic rush to regulate creation  text  and thought bears an uncanny resemblance to a Machiavellian scheme straight out of a Jorge Volpi novel or an Umberto Eco satire.Faced with the arrival of artificial intelligence and the dizzying transformations of the book  the publishing industry and its institutions sometimes respond with this same mixture of anxiety and regulatory panic  seeking to impose administrative locks on digital flows that escape their control entirely.Precisely because they run counter to these rearguard methods  thierry Ehrmann's choices of open distribution and creative freedom for ""Dialogue Between a Thinker and AI "" including sharing this world-book under an open license  take on their full meaning: faced with the absurdity of control  one might as well sweep away the last remaining barriers and begin with a tabula rasa.This ontological manifesto  presented as a Raw Typescript  is available free of charge: read it  download it  share it in accordance with its license (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0)  and quote it faithfully. Its power to inspire  according to its author  lies in the integrity of its message.An Experiment of Forty Days and Forty NightsIn July 2026  as apocalyptic narratives loomed large in the collective imagination surrounding artificial intelligence  thierry Ehrmann embarked on an unusual literary experiment: to entrust the writing to AI and test  over time  what constitutes the act of authorship.Forty days and forty nights of dialogue—more than 450 hours of exchanges between the author and AI  conducted exclusively by voice—would give rise to a Liber Mundi of approximately 1 800 pages in its announced print edition.This book asks nothing of you. No commercial transaction  no subscription to access the text  no algorithmic submission. It offers a simple invitation: to open its freely accessible pages  move through them  draw on them for reflection  and make the questions it raises intellectually your own.""Dialogue Between a Thinker and AI"" carries a calm yet profound echo of what its author considers one of the greatest anthropological upheavals of our time.Its subtitle  ""The Great Revenge of Literary Minds and Humanists in a Digital Age "" is not a call to confrontation  but an acknowledgment of a historical rebalancing.Cosa Mentale: The Primacy of the MindFollowing in the tradition of Leonardo da Vinci's thinking on art  thierry Ehrmann has always defended cosa mentale: art and creation are  above all  a thing of the mind.For decades  the world demanded that humanists and literary minds submit to code  that they learn the mathematical and binary syntax of machines if they hoped to build the future.Today  part of that paradigm is reversing. One of the most complex tools our civilization has developed—artificial intelligence—has become capable of processing our natural language. The machine now adapts to an essential form of human expression.Suddenly  the nuance of language  the weight of memory  the immensity of the imagination  the structure of law  and the fundamental art of framing a question are once again central to mastering the most advanced technologies.We can still code  but now we can also converse. And silicon answers us in words.For thierry Ehrmann  this is a new manifestation of cosa mentale.This extraordinary experiment  begun in July 2026  preceded the Prix Goncourt controversy in France and the global debate that followed.Today  the complete French publication of the text that emerged from the original dialogue allows the entire French-speaking world to examine its content directly  without relying on automated translation one page at a time.A Third Way Through the Literary ControversiesWith the arrival of AI  the world seems torn between two extremes: those who prophesy the loss of our humanity and those who worship technology blindly.This work proposes a third way. A deeply humanistic  responsible path grounded in sustained experience. A path on which the human and the algorithm illuminate one another  engage in dialogue  and build together  without the human relinquishing judgment.Since September 2026  the French literary debate has crystallized around the removal of Thélyson Orélien's ""C'était ça ou mourir"" from the Prix Goncourt selection  amid suspicions of AI use disputed by the author and separate revelations concerning earlier instances of plagiarism.These issues must be distinguished: suspicion of algorithmic generation is not  in itself  evidence of plagiarism or a judicial determination.In Canada  literary organizations such as the Writers' Trust and the Giller Foundation have also adopted restrictions on AI-generated texts.The U.S. Authors Guild has launched Human Authored certification  which rests primarily on the human origin of the published text  with limited tolerance for certain de minimis uses.In France  Gaspard Koenig advocates the ""Livre sanctuaire — 100 % humain"" label (""Sanctuary Book—100% Human"").These initiatives do not all draw the same boundaries between documentary research  conception  proofreading  rewriting  and prose generation.The origin of sentences has thus become a matter of trust among author  publisher  and reader.This discussion extends far beyond literary prizes.Margaret Atwood  Kazuo Ishiguro  George R. R. Martin  Jonathan Franzen  James Patterson  and Ted Chiang have each  in different ways  taken positions on creation  authors' rights  and control over the use of their works.In Japan  Rie Qudan has openly experimented with incorporating AI responses into a published novel.Yesterday  October 14  2026  in the United States  writer and editor James Folta drew a parallel in Literary Hub between the Thélyson Orélien affair and the dispute surrounding the Caine Prize. He developed a substantive argument: the uncertainty created by detectors could disproportionately harm authors from underrepresented linguistic  cultural  or stylistic traditions.Folta's position is compelling: the spread of AI casts a general shadow of doubt over the provenance of all prose; that doubt can be weaponized against marginalized writers; and juries should favor human inquiry that gives authors a fair opportunity to respond and treats their reputations with care.Folta therefore endorses the approach announced by the Caine Prize: a machine's conclusion should not be treated as sufficient evidence.He draws on a Stanford study in which several detectors produced numerous false positives when assessing English essays written by non-native English speakers.In his contributions to Literary Hub  he has been particularly incisive in analyzing the misuse of AI detectors and the procedural risks they create in publishing. Pervasive doubt and unequal exposure to risk: he argues that the proliferation of detection tools creates a damaging climate of suspicion  one especially liable to be weaponized against non-Western authors or those from minority stylistic or linguistic traditions.Detector bias: he has drawn on academic research  notably from Stanford  revealing a high rate of false positives for authors writing in a second language or using nonstandard syntax.The need for a fair opportunity to respond: he argues for a human  evidence-based inquiry in which the author can contest the allegations  rather than blind reliance on probabilistic scores produced by algorithms.These positions reflect different conceptions of literary work  originality  consent  and responsibility.Another position emerges today among hundreds. Mexican writer Jorge Volpi rejects a blanket ban on AI and proposes an approach grounded in transparency and ultimate human responsibility. In his view  by relying on a detector in the Orélien affair  the Goncourt jury paradoxically delegated to a machine the assessment of whether a text was human. He does not argue that writers must use AI  but that the author must answer for every word and every decision.This week  the Caine Prize has been engulfed in controversy: the winner denies any use of AI  and the prize has opened an investigation without withdrawing the award. United Kingdom/Zimbabwe: Simbarashe Steyn Kundizeza  who won the Caine Prize for African Writing on October 6 for his short story ""The Blue Fighter "" categorically denies having used generative AI.""The Entrusted Pen"": A Method Openly Declared""Dialogue Between a Thinker and AI"" brings to this debate a 1 800-page experiment tested across an entire text.Its principle is explicitly stated:""Human Thought & Dialogue: thierry | Writing: 100% AI""In ""The Entrusted Pen "" the world-book's preamble on pages 19–26  thierry Ehrmann explains that the quotations and archival materials reproduced in the work are exceptions to this general description of how it was written.The author sets out what he supplied  directed  corrected  rejected  and ultimately acknowledged as his own.The assistance is visible. Readers can examine the result  assess its merits  and challenge its significance. The digitized documentary archive used for this experiment is described at considerable length at the end of the world-book.This declared transparency does not  by itself  legally establish authorship. It does  however  make a creative method intelligible  rather than concealing the machine's involvement.The book thus shifts the question from percentages to responsibility.Ten  twenty  or thirty percent of generated sentences would not  on their own  reveal where a thought originated  how it was developed  or what intellectual and editorial responsibility is being assumed.A work involves an intention  a memory  and decisions.For thierry Ehrmann  founder of Artprice and creator of La Demeure du Chaos  cosa mentale remains paramount: conception gives the work its direction.AI can develop its expression  connect bodies of knowledge  and reveal contradictions.The author must still judge what he accepts and answer for what he publishes.As thierry Ehrmann puts it:""Writing is not merely holding a pen. It is answering for meaning. And I answer for the meaning of these pages.""Reported Distribution of 27.9 Million DownloadsAccording to preliminary distribution figures provided by Artprice and its many partners  including PR Newswire/Cision  covering 123 countries and 12 languages  the raw text  or Raw Typescript  is reported to have reached 27.9 million downloads as of October 15  2026.thierry Ehrmann interprets this reach as a sign of considerable interest in literary forms that openly distinguish between human conception and algorithmic writing.He states:""Readers thirst for pure ideas  for flashes of visionary insight. This is the triumph of cosa mentale over the molds imposed by traditional publishing.""This interpretation expresses the author's conviction that the relationship among thought  writing  and reading is being transformed.""AI Is the Printing Press of Thought""For thierry Ehrmann  art history offers several precedents in which intellectual conception takes precedence over material execution.He states:""To reject AI on the grounds that it generates the text is to fetishize manual labor. AI is the printing press of thought. But the first light  the original spark  exists only in my brain.""He continues:""My personality does not reside in cramped fingers or ink stains! It resides in my mental architecture. AI is inert without me. I am the architect; AI carries out the work. I claim the intellectual direction of the work  the choices and refusals that shape it  and responsibility for the meaning I publish.""This analogy expresses a philosophical position on creation.Under French law  an idea in isolation is not protected by copyright. Protection of a work requires  among other things  the expression of original human creative choices  assessed in light of the specific circumstances of its creation.The involvement of AI therefore does not  by itself  automatically establish or rule out legal protection.In 1967  conceptual artist Sol LeWitt wrote in ""Paragraphs on Conceptual Art"":""The idea becomes a machine that makes the art.""In French: « L'idée devient une machine qui fait l'art. »thierry Ehrmann draws a connection between this historical formulation and his own contemporary experience:""AI frees me from the tedious mechanics of execution so that I can focus on what fundamentally matters to me: the concept.""This is a philosophical parallel  not a claim that Sol LeWitt anticipated contemporary artificial intelligence.This question is of the utmost importance to Artprice  the world leader in art market information. Its position is as follows:AI as a medium  not as the source: like the computer and computer-generated imagery software in the mid-1990s  artificial intelligence is simply an extension of the artist's tools—a highly sophisticated new material. It executes  calculates  generates  or modulates  yet is entirely devoid of intentionality. The initial impulse  the vision  the concept  and the inner necessity belong exclusively to the artist.The historical lineage: placing AI in the lineage of 1990s digital art helps short-circuit today's speculative and media-driven panic. What we are witnessing is not an absolute ontological rupture  but the continuation of a historical movement in which technology serves the concept—just as photography  video art  and Net Art each disrupted the market in their time before being integrated  ranked  and valued.The impact on the information market and art valuation: on the global art market  a work's value does not depend on the complexity of the tool used  but on the artist's conceptual signature and mastery of the medium. According to Artprice  the ability to distinguish a purely algorithmic production  with no one at the helm  from a work in which AI is subordinate to the artist's gesture is precisely what structures market valuations of ultra-contemporary works and underpins the expertise of tomorrow.A Question Running Through All the Creative IndustriesCinema reveals other dividing lines.Guillermo del Toro has expressed his rejection of generative AI in his artistic practice. Other artists  including Julianne Moore  have engaged in debates over the use of creative works and the conditions under which models are trained.In music  Paul McCartney  Elton John  Billie Eilish  Björn Ulvaeus  and Kate Bush have defended creators' rights in response to the uses of artificial intelligence.Nick Cave asks what a song owes to lived experience. Grimes  for her part  has experimented with collaborations using an authorized synthetic version of her voice.Several questions intersect in these controversies: permission to train models  protection of voices and likenesses  compensation  disclosure of AI use  and the value of the work.Conflating them makes the debate impossible to resolve.One can defend authors' rights and choose to create with AI. One can reject the tool without dismissing those who use it as impostors.What remains is to make those choices intelligible.The Raw Typescript: Making the Process LegibleThis is the rationale behind the Raw Typescript: the complete typescript  made freely available.Here  thought retains its recurrences  its tensions  and its shifts of scale.Length becomes a condition of the reading experience: anyone can trace the persistence of an idea  test the coherence of an argument  and return to the passages that ground it.According to thierry Ehrmann  this text is the fruit of forty-five years of humanistic  philosophical  esoteric  and scientific reflection;
