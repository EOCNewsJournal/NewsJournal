The EU sanctioned the NSD in June 2022 as part of unprecedented restrictions imposed on Moscow over the Ukraine conflict. The move left securities held through the institution frozen at European depositories.The dispute centered on an exemption allowing payments under contracts signed before sanctions took effect. The Luxembourg authorities argued that it covered money transfers but not securities. The court disagreed  ruling that the term ‘payment’ is not limited to cash and can also cover the return of securities to their owners.As neither the Russian company nor its clients were sanctioned when the ministry rejected their application in September 2023  the court found that the authorities had wrongly refused to release their assets under the exemption.The decision could help other Russian investors recover their frozen assets by challenging the way EU sanctions have been interpreted. However  the ruling does not automatically unfreeze the assets. Any transfers must still comply with the broader EU sanctions  including a requirement that fees paid to the NSD remain frozen.The judgment does not identify the Russian company or its clients. It concerns private investments  not Russia’s frozen sovereign reserves  and does not overturn the broader EU sanctions regime.In a separate development  Finnish authorities have suspended the seizure of Russian state assets following a ruling by the Helsinki Court of Appeal  Helsingin Sanomat reported on Friday. The dispute concerns the assets nationalized by the Crimean authorities after the peninsula voted to join Russia in a 2014 referendum.
